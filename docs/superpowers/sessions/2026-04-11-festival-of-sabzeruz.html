Festival of Sabzeruz

A chess master in Port Ormos, a lonely girl in a looping city, and the slow realization that both were already ten moves ahead of you.

Runtime: ~4 hours | Scenes: 10 | Combats: 3 | Puzzles: 1 (loop-pattern) | Player Choices: 5

Source: Through Mists of Smoke and Forests Dark (Scenes 7-9) + The Morn a Thousand Roses Brings (openings through loop 3 / investigation start)

Load-bearing beats (do not cut): (1) Alhaitham's Dori setup + the duel contingency; (2) Alhaitham walking off with the Capsule; (3) The first "reset" moment where the party realizes the day restarted; (4) Nahida's "dook dook"; (5) The cliffhanger reveal that something bigger is happening.

Scene 1 — Port Ormos, Before the Duel

0:00-0:25 RP

The stink of tar and cardamom rolls off the water. Port Ormos crowds the dockside like a patient that refuses to lie still — merchants shouting over one another in six languages, half-furled sails slapping masts, and beneath it all the soft hum of the Akasha in every passing ear, including yours. The terminals at your temples have been chiming all morning with snippets of dock gossip you didn't ask for. You came to Ormos for one thing: to find a man named Alhaitham, retrieve a report he is supposedly writing, and bring it back. You leave the inn that morning to the sound of him already finishing the conversation you came to start. He is in the courtyard outside the harbormaster's office, calmly closing a book on three members of Ayn Al-Ahmar, who walk away from him with the careful steps of people who have just agreed to something they now regret.
About the Akasha Terminals: The party is wearing them. This was given to them in the previous session. The terminals chime with passive trivia — let one or two players hear something useful out of context (a passing dockworker's family name, a price they should expect to pay, etc.). It is ambient flavor. The terminals are also why Alhaitham can find them so easily — the Akasha logs every wearer's location.

DM Beats

  • Alhaitham just wrapped up the public argument with the Eremites. A duel is set for "a few days" — the clock is running.
  • The party saw the tail end. Let them approach him. He does not look up from his book.
  • He already knows they are coming for him. Establish this immediately — he uses one of their names before they give it, and he names their mission before they state it.
  • The "report" the party was sent to retrieve is, in fact, his ongoing investigation into the Divine Knowledge Capsule. The party does not know this yet. Alhaitham knows they don't know.
"You came from The Chasm. You walked through Gandharva Ville. Tighnari is not known for introducing himself twice, so I will assume you are not here to waste my afternoon. You want the report. I have not finished writing it. You can have it tonight, or you can have it accurate — choose. While you are choosing, there is a small thing you can do for me that will make the version you receive accurate. There is a woman in this city named Dori. She is the only person in Ormos who refuses, on principle, to sell anything to a Sage of the Akademiya. I am, regrettably, on her list. You are not. Now — shall we agree to be useful to each other, or shall I find other tools?"
Show him knowing. When a player asks "how did you —", interrupt them mid-sentence with the answer. When they ask why he's helping, say: "I'm not. You're helping me. You simply haven't realized yet that the terms are favorable to you." Then return to his book.

Working With Alhaitham

Setup: He will hand over a complete, accurate version of the report — but only after the Dori errand. The "incomplete tonight" version is real; it will lack the one critical piece he hasn't confirmed yet.

  • Agree to the errand → He nods once, gives them a coin purse (2,000 mora) and an address. No warmth. "Do not haggle." Unlocks Scene 2. He'll deliver the full report after the duel.
  • Demand the incomplete report now → He closes the book and slides over a sealed scroll case. "Twenty pages of context, no conclusion. You will return for the conclusion within the hour. I'll be at Dori's anyway." Same outcome, but he docked them for the inconvenience — they get the full report later, but he is colder.
  • Refuse and investigate alone → He shrugs. They waste 30 minutes of in-game time bumping into dead ends — nobody else in Ormos is writing this report, and the Akasha cannot answer questions Alhaitham hasn't filed yet. They circle back and take the deal anyway. Persistent impact: Alhaitham's respect for them drops a notch. In the Scene 5 duel he will not warn them about the berserk.

Persistent impact: Whichever option they pick, mark it on your prep sheet. It affects Alhaitham's willingness to hint later.

Scene 2 — Being Dori's Customer

0:25-0:55 RP

The address leads to a shaded courtyard behind the bazaar. A woman sits cross-legged on a cushion the color of bruised plums, counting coins onto a tray with the practiced boredom of someone who has counted a great many coins. She looks up. Her eyes do a little accounting of their own. "Oh? Oh — little friends. Come in, come in. Tell Auntie Dori what a handsome sum is doing on such tired shoulders."

DM Beats — The Errand

  • Dori does not deal with Sages on principle — but the party is not Sages, and she can see the Akasha Terminals on them, which means they are Akademiya-adjacent without being of the Akademiya. That is exactly the loophole Alhaitham was counting on.
  • Dori offers absurd luxury items: a "genuine" Mondstadt Cecilia pressed in amber, a vial of "distilled moonlight," a beetle in a jar she swears is a reincarnated poet.
  • Alhaitham said: do not haggle. Emphasize the discomfort — the players are spending someone else's money on obvious junk, and something about it feels like a test.
  • One player is expected to eventually ask Dori a question while transacting. This is the play. The question Alhaitham needs answered is "Who has the Divine Knowledge Capsule?" — but he will not have told them that. They need to either guess, fish, or be guided.

What to Buy From Dori

Setup: They have 2,000 mora and strict instructions. They must buy something. Alhaitham did not tell them what to ask.

  • The amber Cecilia (800m) → Useless purchase. Dori warms up slightly. They walk back to Alhaitham with junk and no information. He sighs once, audibly, then asks them what they actually asked her. They will realize they were supposed to ask a question.
  • The "moonlight vial" (1,200m) → Dori chuckles. "A connoisseur!" Warms more. Same outcome — they didn't ask the right question.
  • Ask Dori what she knows about the report Alhaitham is writing → Dori freezes, then smiles. "You're his little friends, aren't you. Of course. Of course you are. He's chasing the same thing the desert fanatics are chasing. The shiny one. Tell him the answer is Mizri — and the price is the whole purse." This was what Alhaitham wanted all along.
  • Ask Dori about the Eremites / the duel / Ayn Al-Ahmar directly → She considers it. "Mmm. Same answer, smaller price. Mizri's the one you want. He's been buying things he shouldn't be able to afford, with knowledge he shouldn't be able to have. 1,500 mora and we never had this conversation."

Persistent impact: If they don't figure out they're supposed to ask a question, Dori eventually leans in and says "Ah — or did he not tell you what to ask? Dear dear. Let Auntie help." She spoon-feeds it for the full purse. Whichever path they take, they leave with the name Mizri.

The reveal — play this for maximum "oh.": When they return to Alhaitham and report the name, he does not thank them. He hands over the now-finished report — a thin folio of maybe twelve pages with an addendum slip clipped to the front that wasn't there an hour ago. He simply says the line below, and the DM should actually pause and wait for the players' reaction before continuing.
"Good. Here is your report. The conclusion is on the addendum, which I just finished writing. Dori does not lie to paying customers — a point of pride. She would have lied to me. I needed her to say the name out loud, to someone, while sober. Now she has, and now I can put it in the report I am giving you, which is the report you came here for, which is also the report I needed her to make possible. You are very useful. Thank you for your 2,000 mora. I'll reimburse it from the Akademiya's discretionary fund next week. Shall we go to the duel?"
What's in the report (for the DM to summarize if a player reads it): Twelve pages of Akasha-cross-referenced research on the Divine Knowledge Capsule — its theorized function (a forbidden bridge to knowledge above the Akasha tier), its history (Akademiya-suppressed, blamed on rogue Haravatat scholars), and a list of suspected current holders. The addendum slip simply reads: "Confirmed by Dori, Port Ormos, this morning: Mizri of Ayn Al-Ahmar." The party now realizes the report they were sent to fetch is not a normal report — and the person who sent them on this errand may not have known what they were asking for.

Scene 3 — Walking to the Duel Grounds

0:55-1:10 RP LORE

DM Beats — Show the Chess Master

  • As they walk, Alhaitham lists, unprompted, the exact order in which the fight will go wrong.
  • He points out three Eremite lookouts the party hadn't noticed. "Two. Four. There. Don't look. They've been watching us since the fountain."
  • A player will probably ask how he knows Mizri will go berserk. He answers, cleanly, like it's obvious.
"The Capsule is not a weapon. Ayn Al-Ahmar believe it is. When a man who believes in a thing finally holds the thing, and the thing disappoints him, he does one of two things. He weeps, or he breaks. Mizri does not weep. So he will break, and when he breaks he will reach for the Akasha because that is the only power he still trusts, and the Akasha will not save him because the Akasha was never his. That is when I will sever his terminal. Please stand to the left of the third pillar when it happens. The fragments fly right."
Correcting their assumptions. If a player says "so you're going to steal the Capsule after?" — Alhaitham does not confirm or deny. He simply says: "I am going to do what is necessary." Make a mental note of whichever player asked. In Scene 6 he will look at that player specifically when he walks away with it.

Scene 4 — The Duel Grounds

1:10-1:50 COMBAT

The duel ground is an old sunstone quarry south of Ormos — three broken pillars, a floor of cracked white tile, wind tugging at a dozen Eremite banners. Mizri stands at the center with the Capsule already in his fist. He is speaking, but you cannot hear him over the blood in your ears. Alhaitham, three paces ahead of you, quietly says: "Left of the third pillar. Now."

Encounter — The Duel of Ayn Al-Ahmar

Setup: Alhaitham fights Mizri 1v1 in the center. The party is supposed to handle Mizri's lieutenants so the duel stays clean. It will not stay clean.

Enemies: 1x Eremite Ravenbeak Halberdier [Rare] (Mizri, locked to Alhaitham in Phase 1), 2x Eremite Sword Dancer [Common], 1x Eremite Linebreaker [Uncommon]

Phase 1 (Rounds 1-2): Party vs. the lieutenants. Alhaitham and Mizri exchange blows in the background; describe Alhaitham calmly parrying and reading as he fights.

Phase 2 (Round 3): Mizri cracks the Capsule, screams, and goes berserk. His tier effectively ticks up — treat him as Epic for this round only. He attacks his own surviving lieutenants first. This is the "it's happening now" moment.

Phase 3 (Round 4+): On Alhaitham's turn he severs Mizri's Akasha Terminal ("Left of the third pillar. Now."). Mizri collapses. If any party member is actually standing in that spot, they take no damage from the shrapnel; anyone not there eats 1d8 piercing.

Goal: Survive to Phase 3 without letting a lieutenant reach Alhaitham.

Twist: As Mizri falls, he whispers — loud enough only for the Traveler-proxy / lore-carrying PC to hear — "...World... forget me..." The same words from the red-sky vision in the cave. Say nothing more about it. Let it hang.

Reward: Story progression; the Matra arrive and take Mizri away.

Scene 5 — Alhaitham Walks Away With It

1:50-2:05 RP

The Matra take Mizri. The lieutenants are dead, or cuffed, or fled. Alhaitham closes his book — you didn't even see him open it again — and walks past the broken tile to where the Capsule lies in the dust. He picks it up. He dusts it off on his sleeve. He puts it in an inner pocket. Then, as if remembering that he shares the world with other people, he turns to you.
"An informative afternoon. The Capsule contains no divine knowledge and no divine power — what it contains is a particular kind of madness, the same madness that breaks scholars who probe Irminsul too deeply. I suspected as much, but I needed a zealot to confirm it. The report you carry is now true in every line. Treat it accordingly. You came to Sumeru looking for the Dendro Archon. I cannot help you with that. She is inaccessible. Go to the Sabzeruz Festival and make your other inquiries there. One last thing —" (he looks at whichever player asked about stealing the Capsule earlier) "— you were right about the question. You were wrong about the answer."
This is the moment. Open the pre-sealed envelope marked OPEN WHEN PLAYERS ASK WHY ALHAITHAM USED THEM. Inside: "Dori paid the real price." Hand it to a player. Do not explain. Let them work out that the whole errand — the coins, the haggle-ban, the question — was Alhaitham building a record-able transaction that makes Dori legally complicit, so she cannot later testify against him without destroying her own business. He did not use the party. He used Dori. The party was the invoice.

Confront Alhaitham?

Setup: The envelope has been opened. The party realizes.

  • Call him out publicly → He nods: "Yes. Is there a question inside the accusation?" No apology. He walks off. Party gains +1 "Alhaitham owes us" marker for a future session.
  • Let him walk → He pauses, just once, and says "Thank you for the professional courtesy." This is the closest thing to respect he gives. +1 "Alhaitham is impressed" marker.
  • Try to take the Capsule from him → He does not resist. He hands it over. "Open it." (He knows they won't. If they do — roll a WIS save, DC 16, on fail the PC sees the red-sky vision and is dazed for 1 round.) Either way, he says: "Now do you understand why I was going to keep it?" and holds out his hand. If they give it back, he leaves with approval. If not, he leaves without — and the party now carries a cursed artifact they don't understand.

Persistent impact: Note which ending you got. It affects his next appearance.

Scene 6 — Sumeru City, The Festival Morning

2:05-2:30 RP

Sumeru City wakes in gold. The Divine Tree's leaves throw dappled light across the Grand Bazaar and the air smells of saffron, citrus, and hot sugar. Banners in purple and green hang from every balcony. Dunyarzad meets you at the edge of the bazaar with a tired smile and a guest list in her hand. "You came," she says, like it matters that you did. "Today is going to be lovely."

DM Beats — Establish the "Normal" Day

  • Dunyarzad walks them through three festival venues. Hit each briefly:
    1. Vihar's Yalda Candy stall — mystery box flavors. Let a player pick a box blind. Describe the flavor. (Remember it. It matters in loop 2.)
    2. The garland weavers — a quiet station where an old man weaves Padisarah replicas from purple-dyed silk. He complains they don't look right. The color is wrong.
    3. The performance stage — Nilou is warming up. She is radiant and terrified.
  • A small, barefoot girl in a white dress watches the party from three stalls away. When they look, she's gone. Do this twice — never three times. (She is Nahida. Not yet revealed.)
  • En route to the stage, Eremite mercenaries ambush the group trying to grab Dunyarzad. Dehya appears.

Encounter — Kidnapping Attempt

Setup: A narrow alley between the bazaar and the bathhouse. Dehya holds the alley mouth, the party defends Dunyarzad in the middle, civilians at the far end.

Enemies: 3x Eremite Desert Clearwater [Common], 1x Eremite Galehunter [Uncommon]

Goal: Don't let Dunyarzad take any hits. She is terminal and any damage triggers a collapse.

Twist: Halfway through, Dunyarzad's knees buckle anyway — Eleazar flare. The party must decide: finish the fight, or pull her out. Dehya can hold alone if pressed.

Reward: Dehya's trust. A quiet aside from Dunyarzad: "Please don't tell Nilou. Today has to be perfect."

The Stage — Azar Shuts It Down

  • They arrive at the stage to find Grand Sage Azar and Secretary Setaria. Azar cites "eleven regulatory violations." The festival is canceled.
  • Nilou is crushed. Dunyarzad accepts with dignity. The party's choice is to protest, accept, or try to stall.
  • As Azar walks off, he says the line: "Enjoy your celebration of the birth of that god — to your heart's content." Emphasize "that god." It is too strange to be a slip.
  • Somewhere, a faint beeping sound. One player might hear it on a passive Perception 14. Describe it for one heartbeat, then let it fade.

Respond to Azar

Setup: The Grand Sage is shutting down a dying girl's festival.

  • Publicly argue with him → He does not engage. He repeats himself. Setaria logs the party's names.
  • Quietly gather info on him → A bystander says Azar has been "strange lately." Vendor mentions they haven't seen the Dendro Archon in public ever. Useful for loop investigation.
  • Let it go and comfort Dunyarzad → She gives them a small token: a handwritten booklet of Sumeran folk legends. Keep this prop — it matters in a later session.

(At this point the party goes to sleep at Dunyarzad's house. The DM hears the beep. The players do too if they made the passive check. Fade to black.)

Scene 7 — The First Reset

2:30-2:50 RP LORE

How to run the reset. Do not announce it. Re-read the Scene 6 opening — the exact same words, same voice — "Sumeru City wakes in gold. The Divine Tree's leaves throw dappled light..." Keep a straight face. When a player interrupts with "wait — didn't you already —", act mildly confused and keep reading. Let the tension build for 20-30 seconds before breaking.
Sumeru City wakes in gold. The Divine Tree's leaves throw dappled light across the Grand Bazaar and the air smells of saffron, citrus, and hot sugar. Banners in purple and green hang from every balcony. Dunyarzad meets you at the edge of the bazaar with a tired smile and a guest list in her hand. "You came," she says, like it matters that you did. "Today is going to be lovely."

DM Beats — Loop 2

  • Let the players act on their memory. They alone remember. Dunyarzad, Nilou, Vihar, Dehya — all forgot.
  • The Yalda Candy at Vihar's stall — Vihar offers the mystery box. If a player says the flavor before biting it, confirm they are correct. "How did you — oh, lucky guess!" Vihar is delighted. The player is not.
  • The small barefoot girl shows up deliberately this time. She locks eyes with a PC. Then she vanishes behind the weaver's stall.
  • The kidnapping attempt happens again, at the same alley, with the same enemies. Let the party handle it faster or differently. Same outcome: Dunyarzad collapses, Azar cancels the festival, the beep, the dark.
Important sync note. Only the players keep memory between loops. They do not keep HP, positioning, items acquired during a loop, or spells cast. At each reset: heal to full, restore consumables, reset character sheets to their morning-of-festival state. The one thing they keep: knowledge. If they learned something in loop 1, they know it in loop 2. Write player-made discoveries on an index card and keep it face-up on the table.

Scene 8 — Loop 3: Chasing the Girl

2:50-3:15 RP COMBAT

Sumeru City wakes in gold. You know this morning by heart now. You know the smell, the angle of the light, the moment Dunyarzad will say "You came." You also know that somewhere in this bazaar is a girl in a white dress who has been watching you, and today you are not going to miss her.

DM Beats — Breaking Pattern

  • The party can now abandon Dunyarzad and go hunting. Let them. The city will warp around their detour.
  • Describe inconsistencies: a vendor whose stall was in one place yesterday is now two streets over. Two different children wearing the same face. A cat that walks into a wall and phases through it.
  • Chasing the girl leads through a side alley where they're ambushed by Treasure Hoarders — wrong enemies for this part of the city. Optional combat; if the party is tired, skip it and have the hoarders flee.

Encounter — The Wrong Alley (Optional)

Setup: A side alley the party has never seen on previous loops. These enemies don't belong in Sumeru City.

Enemies: 2x Crossbow Treasure Hoarder [Common], 1x Shovel Treasure Hoarder [Common]

Goal: Win or scatter them.

Twist: When defeated, the bodies do not fall. They unravel — crumble into motes of leaf-green light and scatter. The dream is spending computation it didn't budget for.

Reward: Strong evidence this is not the real world.

Meeting the Girl

The chase ends at Bimarstan — the clinic. The girl is sitting cross-legged on a windowsill, humming. She notices the party and does not flinch.

Nahida voice reminder: She is a small, lonely, too-well-read child. Protective instincts should fire. The party MUST NOT know she's the Archon. If a player meta-guesses, play dumb: "She's just a girl. She seems like she's been alone a long time."
She looks up from her humming. Her feet are bare and they don't quite touch the windowsill. "You're the ones who keep seeing me," she says. "I wasn't sure if you would today. Some days you don't. On the days you don't I go home early." She tilts her head, and her eyes track something invisible near your shoulder — a little insect, maybe, or a thought. "There's a beetle on your arm. A green one. He's saying — "

She makes a small, cheerful, rhythmic sound with her mouth.

"Dook. Dook. Dook dook dook."

"That's beetle for 'I am very round and I am going to the sun.' He says it a lot. I think he means it." She looks up again. "You don't remember me, do you? That's all right. I remember you. I remember you every morning."
The "dook dook" line is load-bearing. Deliver it deadpan. Let it land. The player reaction is the point. If any player bursts out laughing, Nahida beams — it is the first time she has made anyone laugh in a very, very long time. That look matters later.

What Nahida Tells Them (Loop 3)

  • She calls the phenomenon a samsara. She is careful with the word.
  • She says the party can see her because of "what you are, and what you're carrying." She will not elaborate.
  • She says she can't tell them the answer, because "if I say it plainly, your head will go —" and she mimes a gentle pop with her hands.
  • She will tell them: "The day is a dream. Somebody is dreaming it. When you find who, you can wake them up, and then it will stop."
  • She does not say who she is. If they ask her name, she says: "You can call me whatever you like. Names get confusing where I come from."

Scene 9 — The Loop Pattern Puzzle

3:15-3:40 PUZZLE

The Pattern of the Festival

DM prep: 6 pre-cut index cards. Each has one symbol written large: FLOWER, CANDLE, BIRD, DRUM, BELL, ROSE.

Setup: Nahida tells the party that every loop, the festival rings six signals in a specific order — she has heard them from her window but cannot remember the pattern, because her memory is "heavier than theirs." If the party can write down the correct order of the six signals across the remaining loop time, they can retroactively "mark" the loop and leave a trace. (This is the hook for next session — the "marker" they leave is what Nahida will use later.)

Tell the players: As they act through the last minutes of Loop 3, the DM will mention signals at specific moments in the narration. At the end of the loop, they must lay the six cards on the table in the correct order, from memory.

The signals the DM must weave into loop 3 narration (in this exact order):

  1. BELL — "The bazaar bell rings noon as you pass the archway."
  2. FLOWER — "A child drops a silk Padisarah at your feet. Purple, but not purple enough."
  3. DRUM — "Somewhere up the hill, a drum begins the festival overture."
  4. BIRD — "A startled dusk bird flies across the plaza, black against the gold."
  5. CANDLE — "Vihar lights the first candle at his stall as the shadows lengthen."
  6. ROSE — "On the stage, Nilou lifts a single white rose above her head. The audience goes still."

Solution: Bell → Flower → Drum → Bird → Candle → Rose. Players must arrange the six cards in this order on the table at the end of the loop. No re-reads.

Hint progression:

  • Hint 1 (free): Nahida says, "Start with the loudest thing."
  • Hint 2: Nahida says, "The flower came before the music. The music came before the wings."
  • Hint 3: Nahida sighs and names the first three in order, leaving the last three to them.

Unlock: When solved, Nahida's eyes widen slightly. "Oh. Oh, that's — that's a very clean pattern. Somebody wrote this dream on purpose. Somebody is composing it." This is the revelation the session hinges on. Mark it with a beat of silence.

If they fail: Nahida is kind about it. "That's all right. Try again tomorrow." She writes a partial pattern on a slip of paper and tucks it into a PC's pocket. Next session starts with them having the partial solution.

Scene 10 — The Beep, And What's Behind It

3:40-4:00 RP LORE

You are back at Dunyarzad's house. She is sleeping. You can hear her breathing. You can also hear — now that you know to listen for it — a small, regular, mechanical beep, coming from somewhere that is not here. It sounds like a heart monitor. It sounds like a countdown. It sounds like it is waiting for you. And then, for the first time since this began, a voice you have never heard before speaks into the dark, and it is not Nahida. It is an old man, calm and certain, and he says: "The project has entered its most critical phase. Jnana Energy output is — stable. Continue."

How Does the Session End?

Setup: The party can choose, in-character, how they spend the final minutes of Loop 3. Pick the ending that best matches what they did this session.

  • They solved the puzzle → End on Nahida's "somebody is composing it." Cut to black. Next session opens on Loop 4 with the party knowing they are in a composition — and looking for the composer.
  • They befriended Nahida deeply → End on Nahida taking one of their hands in both of hers and saying, very seriously, "Thank you for remembering me today. I am going to try to remember you tomorrow, too." She is lying — she remembers every loop. But it is the kindest lie she knows how to tell.
  • They investigated Azar → End on the party standing at the locked gates of the Akademiya, hearing the beep, and realizing the beep comes from inside. Cut to black.
  • They figured out it's not the real world → End on a PC walking to the edge of the city at sunset, looking out at a horizon that does not quite render correctly — the palm trees in the distance are the same palm tree, copied forty times. Cut to black.
  • They got stuck → End on Nahida giving them the partial pattern slip and saying "Tomorrow. We have as many tomorrows as we need. But not more." Cut to black.

Persistent impact: Whichever ending you use, write the last line on an index card and put it in the middle of the table. That is the opening line of next session.

The cliffhanger line, in every ending: after the cut to black, say this out loud once more, quietly, so it's the last thing the players hear before you close the book:

"Sumeru City wakes in gold."

If You Have Extra Time

  • Expand Scene 3 with a roadside puzzle: Alhaitham wordlessly hands a PC a riddle on a torn page and watches them solve it. "You'll need this later." (He's right. He won't say when.)
  • Introduce a second sighting of the small girl during Scene 6 — she trades a single Padisarah petal for a coin, and the petal is the right shade of purple. Let them keep it.
  • Add a brief RP beat with Katheryne at the Adventurers' Guild — she speaks too casually, and something about her eyes is wrong. (Setup for "Dawn" in a future session.)
