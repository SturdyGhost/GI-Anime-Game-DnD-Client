Dreams, Emptiness, Deception

Sumeru Archon Quest — Chapter III, Act III. The party meets a Harbinger they cannot fight, watch Nahida choose them over herself, and limp into the desert with three of the most dangerous people in Sumeru as their only escort. By the time they reach Aaru Village, four people who do not trust each other have to sit in one room and tell the truth.

Players: Brian F. (archer), Brian C. (fighter), Dylan (Kamisato Ayaka).

Runtime: ~4 hours total, designed in three breakable parts — can be run as a single sitting or split across 2–3 sessions.

Scenes: 14 across 3 parts | Combats: 2 (+ 1 dramatic non-combat parry) | Player choices: 6 | Death roll: 1 (Ayaka)

Source: Like a Triumphant Hero (Act III, Quest 1) → The Gaze From a Certain God (Quest 2 — heavily cut) → The Four at Loggerheads (Quest 3).

Tone: Optimism → betrayal → choking dread → (potentially) grief → uneasy alliance under a sandstorm roof → cliffhanger.

Load‑bearing beats (do not cut): (1) Setaria flips to their side and is then captured offstage. (2) The crowd at the Akademiya gate cheers and forces the party back — no combat, only pressure. (3) Dottore introduces himself and says "do not underestimate me." (4) The PLAYER CHOICE on whether to heed him. (5) If they fight: Dylan rolls a single d20 for Ayaka's life. (6) Either way: Nahida steps between them and Dottore and is taken. (7) Tighnari, Dehya, and Alhaitham intercept the fleeing party and escort them to Caravan Ribat. (8) The four‑way standoff at Aaru: Cyno strikes, Alhaitham parries, Dehya mediates, Candace closes the door. (9) Each of the three (Cyno, Alhaitham, Dehya) tells their truth in the hut. (10) Isak asks them to find his grandfather.

Custom branches from canon: The Gandharva Ville / Collei / Pardis Dhyai / Haypasia / Scaramouche‑vision arc is cut entirely. The party never meets Tighnari at home; instead, Tighnari, Dehya, and Alhaitham spot them fleeing Sumeru City and intercept them on the road. Dottore's "do not underestimate me" line is non‑canon — it exists to gate the player choice. Ayaka's death roll is non‑canon — canon Ayaka is not in this scene at all.

  • ☐ The Documents Envelope (PROP). Take a small plain envelope and write "For the Mercenary" on it in pen. Inside, place 3 small slips of paper with the following text (printed or handwritten):
    • Slip 1: "Mass Dream Harvest — Phase 2 outline. Vector: Akasha Terminal v3.2. Sample size: all of Sumeru City. Lead: Grand Sage Azar. Co‑lead: redacted (External, †)." The dagger symbol is just a dagger; the party never gets to ask what it means.
    • Slip 2: "Sanctuary of Surasthana — access protocols updated. The Lesser Lord remains under observation. Forbidden Knowledge Capsule inventory unchanged: 6 of 6 sealed."
    • Slip 3: "Pardis Dhyai — resource diversion approved. Subject H. progressing. Connection has been... unstable. Sign of breakthrough or sign of contamination?"
    Hand the envelope to whichever PC takes the lead at the Akademiya gate, seconds before Dottore appears. Let them open it and read aloud. The mob descends before they finish slip 3. The slips never see the light again. They are evidence the party physically held and then never got to use.
  • ☐ The Death Roll d20 (PROP). Set a separate, distinct d20 aside at the start of the session on the DM's side of the table. Black, red, or otherwise visually different from the players' dice. Do not explain what it's for. If a player asks, smile and don't answer. When Dottore says "do not underestimate me," slide it across the table to Dylan and say nothing. Each PC writes one name (Alhaitham / Cyno / Dehya / Candace / no one) for who they trust most after the hut conversation. DM gathers and reads silently. Result affects future NPC attitudes — see Scene 12.
  • ☐ Re‑read the Cast section — Cyno's voice and Dottore's voice are doing most of the heavy lifting in their respective scenes. Dottore is friendly, conversational, and not at all theatrical — that is what makes him terrifying.
  • ☐ Remember: The party walked out of last session having broken the Sabzeruz Samsara and saved Dunyarzad. They think they've already paid for the right to win. Tonight, the rules change.
  • ☐ Remember: The party will end tonight knowing that the Akademiya, the Fatui, and at least one Harbinger are aligned against them; that Nahida is captured (and likely Ayaka with her, on a bad roll); that Alhaitham was originally tasked by the sages to investigate them; that Cyno is exiled by choice; that Dehya is now between jobs. What they will not learn tonight: where Nahida is actually being held; what the Sanctuary of Surasthana contains; what the "external co‑lead" on the Mass Dream Harvest is; who Scaramouche is; why the Pardis Dhyai entry mentions "contamination." Keep those sealed.

PART ONE — TRIUMPHANT

Target runtime: 80–90 min. Six scenes. No combat. One device‑driven set piece. One death roll. One sacrifice. This part is the emotional spine of the night. A natural pause point sits after Scene 6.

Scene 1 — Dawn (the morning after)

0:00–0:08 RP

Sumeru City the morning after the dream broke is, somehow, exactly the city you walked into the first time. The students are arguing about which staircase is shortest. The bazaar smells of cardamom and warm flatbread. Lambad is shouting at a delivery boy about the freshness of the river fish. The sun comes up over the Akademiya the way the sun has been coming up over the Akademiya for nine hundred and forty‑eight years.

You find Dunyarzad sitting on a bench in front of the Adventurers' Guild, in a clean blue robe, with her hair tied up. She is eating a piece of fruit. She looks at you and her face does the thing faces do when someone is trying not to cry from relief.

"You're back," she says. "Hehe. Sorry, was I asleep that long? I have such a headache."

Pace: 4–6 minutes max. This scene exists to give the players the feeling of victory before tonight ruins it. Let them banter. Let them be smug. Let them buy candy. Do not let them shop for more than two minutes.

If a player wants to tell Dunyarzad what really happened — the loops, the dream, Nilou, Nahida — she does not understand most of it. She laughs in the right places, which is worse than if she had understood, because she trusts them. Do not over‑explain. Move on.

If they ask after Nilou: Nilou is still asleep. The Zubayr Theater is closed for one day in her honor. She is fine.

(Katheryne walks over with a wooden tray of pastries and sits down across from the party. Her smile is unmistakably Nahida's.)

"Good morning. I needed two days to recover, and I needed to talk to you somewhere the sages aren't watching. The Akademiya is not finished with us. Now that they can't run the Sabzeruz scheme again, they will try something else, and we need a mole inside before they do."

"There's a woman named Setaria. Grand Sage Azar's secretary. She's a genius. She's from the desert. She hates herself a little, every day, for working here. If we can make her stop pretending that doesn't matter, she'll help us."

"I've borrowed three minds she knows. People she trusts. Today, you're going to wear their faces, and you're going to push her until she breaks. Don't be cruel. Be honest. The truth is what she's running from."

The setup for Scene 2: The party will inhabit three of Setaria's acquaintances. Nahida supplies the names and the connections. The party rolls Persuasion/Insight/Deception as they like — whatever they roll, Setaria's wall cracks a little more each conversation. There is no "fail" outcome here. She breaks by the end of the third conversation. The scenes vary in how she breaks.

Scene 2 — Wearing Other People's Faces

0:08–0:30 RP

Nahida hands you three small wooden tokens, each carved with a different sigil. "When you hold one of these and look at Setaria, she sees the face I've borrowed. She doesn't see you. She hears your voice as theirs. You have one conversation per token. After that, the spell collapses."

"Akim is at the Port Ormos pier in fifteen minutes. Qishan is in the attic of Lambad's tavern — though the tavern does not, technically, have an attic. Rima is on patrol at the lower terrace of the Akademiya road."

"Push her. Don't break her. There is a difference."

Run this as three short vignettes, ~5–7 minutes each. Each player can take the lead on one if they want, or rotate. Each conversation is a wedge:

  • As Akim (the Port Ormos friend) — push the "you grew up with us in the desert; why are you ashamed of King Deshret now?" angle. Setaria deflects. She tries to change the subject to Akim's father's health. Let the party twist the knife by getting one detail wrong about the father (his arm vs. his leg) — Setaria notices and her composure slips for the first time.
  • As Qishan (the Vahumana scholar) — reveal that "you" have been celebrating the Sabzeruz Festival "so many times you lost count." Setaria stops cold. "That should be impossible. Nothing in the report indicated anything like that..." She has just confirmed there is a report. Don't lampshade this; let the players notice.
  • As Rima (the Corps of Thirty mercenary) — this is where she finally cracks. Speak quietly. Tell her that the desert that belongs to her lies elsewhere. Tell her it's not too late. Don't push, don't shout — let her hear it.

End of Scene 2: Setaria, voice steady for the first time all morning, agrees to steal the project documents and meet the party at the Akademiya gate tomorrow evening. She insists on the gate — she says she can distract the guards more easily on her own ground. She does not know this is a trap. Neither does the party.

How does the party push Setaria?

  • Cruel and effective. They use the worst details Nahida supplied — her father's illness, her abandoned letters home, her shame. She agrees, but visibly hates them a little. Future encounters: cooperative, but cold.
  • Honest and slow. They tell her the truth about Sumeru, the Akademiya, the harvested dreams. She breaks more gently. Future encounters: trusts the party, will pass them small risks.
  • Use Rima to threaten her. They imply King Deshret followers are watching her. She agrees out of fear. Future encounters: scared of the party, unreliable under pressure.

Persistent impact: Setaria is captured offstage tonight — she won't be on the page again for a while. But her future cooperation is set now, and the version of her the party gets back depends on how they wore the masks.

Scene 3 — The Quiet Streets

0:30–0:38 RP

The next night. The streets of Sumeru City are wrong.

You've been here long enough now to know what an evening looks like — the bazaar lanterns coming up one by one, the students arguing across staircases, the smell of grilled fish from Lambad's. Tonight, the lanterns are lit but no one is under them. The bazaar stalls are open but unmanned. A single cat picks its way across a rooftop and stops to look at you.

Katheryne — Nahida — walks beside you. She is, you notice, holding her hands a little too still.

"Where is everyone? It's not late enough for this. Something is..." (she trails off, looking up the slope toward the Akademiya gate) "... wrong."

"Let's keep going. Setaria will be at the meeting point. If something has happened to her, we need to know. But —" (she looks at you, very seriously, and her voice goes very quiet) "— if I tell you to run, you run. You don't ask why. Understood?"

Pace: 5–8 minutes. Build pressure with absence. Every detail the players ask about — the shops, the guards, the cat — comes back as "empty," "empty," "watching."

If they want to abort and turn back: Nahida agrees this is suspicious but says the documents are worth the risk. If any player makes a Perception or Insight check (DC 13), they notice the faint sound of cheering, very far away, coming from up the slope toward the Akademiya. They do not yet know it is for them.

Scene 4 — The Hero's Welcome

0:38–0:55 RP

You climb the last turn of the road toward the Akademiya gate. The cheering stops being faint.

The plaza in front of the gate — usually empty at night, with two bored guards leaning on their halberds — is full.

Not full in the way a market is full. Full in the way a riot is full. Hundreds of faces. Maybe thousands. The plaza, the staircases up to it, the side streets, the rooftops — people are leaning over balcony railings to see you. As the front of the crowd realizes you've arrived, a roar goes up. Not anger. Adoration.

You see faces you know in there. The flatbread vendor from the bazaar. The boy who delivers fish to Lambad. A student you sat next to during the festival. The dockworker who pointed you at Alhaitham on the day you first walked into Sumeru. They are all looking at you with the kind of love people usually reserve for someone they have lost and gotten back.

And they are walking toward you.

Set the Documents Envelope on the table now. Slide it in front of whichever PC took point in Scene 2. Say only: "Setaria left this at the meeting point. She is not here. The crowd is between you and the gate. The envelope is in your hand."

Let them open it. Let them read all three slips aloud. The pace of their reading is the pace of the crowd closing. They will not finish slip 3 before someone in the front row tries to touch the lead PC's face.

When slip 3 is read — "Pardis Dhyai — resource diversion approved. Subject H. progressing. Connection has been... unstable. Sign of breakthrough or sign of contamination?" — that is when the voice comes from behind the crowd.

The crowd parts.

A man walks down the ramp from the gate. He is taller than you expected, in a long, layered coat the color of dried blood, with a half‑mask of fine porcelain covering everything above his nose. The mask is smiling. He is, apparently, also smiling underneath it — you can hear it in his voice before you can see his face.

"Ah," he says, as if welcoming you to a dinner party. "The triumphant hero returns at last. And to a rather spectacular welcome, even if I do say so myself."

Nahida, beside you, goes very still.

"You're..." she says, slowly. "The outcast. Expelled from the Akademiya. The one even the sages were afraid of."

"Indeed, I am," the man agrees, pleasantly. "Although these days they tend to call me —"

(Pause. Let the silence sit. Then, with no theatricality at all:)

"The Doctor."

(Beat. Then, conversationally:)

"Il Dottore. Omega Build. The Second of the Eleven Fatui Harbingers."

"Oh — and if you're looking for your researcher friend, she has already been taken into confinement. With some basic caution, she could have discovered the listening device on her person. Clearly, she lacks the degree of rigor expected of a true scholar. A shame. I was rather looking forward to meeting her."

Voice direction for Dottore: friendly. Conversational. Slightly amused. The voice of a man explaining a chemistry demonstration to a class he likes. NOT theatrical. NOT raised. NOT smug. He never sneers. He chuckles — once — when he explains the trick. That's it.

If a player tries to ask Dottore a question, he answers. Plainly. Briefly. He is enjoying this and sees no reason to hide it. If they accuse him of anything, he agrees: "Yes, that's correct." He doesn't argue.

"The people of Sumeru City... what have you done to them?"

"Oh, this? A small thing. I simply made some minor adjustments to their Akasha Terminals. They can now deposit information directly into the subconscious. As you can see, all these lovely people now believe..." (he gestures, almost shyly, at the crowd) "...that this Traveler is a hero who has just saved the world."

(small laugh)

"Hehe. My experiment is a success."

"And now — it seems they can no longer hold back their sheer adoration."

The crowd is on you. Not yet violent — not yet anything that could be called violent. But there are too many of them. They are reaching for your clothes. The flatbread vendor's daughter, the one Brian C. once tipped, is trying to hold his hand and is crushing his fingers without realizing it. Someone behind Brian F. is sobbing and pulling at his quiver strap. A man in front of Dylan, weeping, kneels and presses his forehead against her boot. Three more press in behind him.

You cannot draw weapons here. These are not enemies. The plaza behind you is already filling with more. There is no way out that does not go through someone.

The Crowd Pressure beat — run as theatre, not combat.

  • Each PC describes one thing they try to do to not hurt anyone. (Examples: shoving gently, climbing a stall, going low and crawling, telling the crowd to back off, freezing in place.)
  • Each PC rolls a single d20 + their best non‑combat skill (Athletics, Acrobatics, Persuasion, etc.). DC 14.
  • Success: the PC opens a small bubble of space around themselves. Long enough to breathe. Not long enough to get out.
  • Failure: they are being moved by the crowd. Backward, toward Dottore. Carried.
  • This is the moment. Dottore is now ten feet from them, hands behind his back, watching. Set the death roll d20 in front of him on the table. Do not explain it.

If a PC tries to draw a weapon: Nahida grabs their wrist. "No. They're civilians. We cannot." She says it quietly.

Scene 5 — "Do Not Underestimate Me"

0:55–1:25 CHOICE

(Dottore walks forward through the crowd, who part for him as if he is the air and they are the wind. He stops six feet from the party. He bows, slightly — just at the neck.)

"I will spare you the introductions you have already heard. We may end this two ways. Either you come with me to the Akademiya quietly, and I make sure none of these dear people are hurt during the transport. Or you resist, and I make a different demonstration."

(He pauses. He looks at each of them in turn.)

"Do not underestimate me."

(He says it the way someone tells you the soup is hot. Then he waits.)

This is the gate. Stop. Look at the players. Do not hurry them. Let the table go silent for as long as it needs to.

If they ask Nahida what to do, she does not answer — she is watching Dottore. She does not know yet what she will do; she's waiting on them.

If they ask each other what to do, let them argue in‑character.

When they decide, present the choice formally:

The party's response to Dottore.

  • A — Heed the warning. They tell Dottore they understand. They lower their hands. They stand still. → Branch H (Heed). Nahida acts.
  • B — Fight him. Any aggressive action — bow drawn, knuckles up, Ayaka's blade out of the sheath. → Branch F (Fight). Dylan rolls.
  • C — Stall. They keep him talking. (Subskill: any check, DC 15, to buy time.) → On success, Nahida gets one extra heartbeat to act, and they roll into Branch H slightly cleaner — the party knows Nahida is about to do something before she does it. On failure, Dottore loses patience and they roll into Branch F.

Persistent impact — this is the most important choice of the night. Branch H means Nahida's sacrifice is clean and the party walks out grieving but whole. Branch F means Dylan picks up the d20 and rolls for Ayaka's life, and the party walks out (probably) with a body or a wound that does not heal in one rest.

Branch H — They Heed the Warning

You lower your hands. Slowly. The crowd around you keeps pressing — they don't know about the conversation that just happened; they're still in their own loop, still adoring you.

Dottore tilts his head. "Wise. I had hoped for that. Now —"

"— no."

(That was Nahida's voice. Quietly. From between you and him.)

(Katheryne — Nahida — has stepped forward. She is, you notice, smaller than the smallest of you. She is also standing directly between the four of you and the Second of the Fatui Harbingers, and she has not turned around to look at you yet.)

(Without turning around.)

"Leave now. You need to get out of here. I will hold them — all of them — and I will deal with him as long as I can. The road south is clear behind you. Don't argue with me. Don't look back."

(A beat. Quieter.)

"Nor can I abandon... the people of Sumeru."

(Another beat. The faintest smile, audible in her voice.)

"Don't worry. We'll meet again outside of the city."

The crowd freezes. Hundreds of them, all at once, like someone has muted the world. The flatbread vendor's daughter loosens her grip on Brian C.'s fingers. The man weeping at Dylan's boot blinks once, twice, and his shoulders relax.

Behind you, the road opens. The press is gone. You can run.

You see, for a second, Dottore looking past Nahida at you with a sudden, sharpened interest — not at you, you realize. At her.

"I see," he says, conversational again, but quieter. "You appear to have overridden their mental faculties with your own consciousness. To possess such a powerful mind, you must be... the God of Wisdom."

Nahida does not answer him.

"Run," she says, again. To you.

Run the escape as cinematic: the party turns and goes. They do not get to roll for it. They do not get to attack Dottore. They do not get to wait. As they run, the last thing they hear is Dottore's voice — not angry, not raised — saying, distantly, "How fascinating. I think we will keep you."

Skip to Scene 6 — Aftermath.

Branch F — They Fight

Three things happen almost at the same time.

Brian F.'s arrow leaves the bow. Brian C. takes one step forward, into a stance that has won him every fight he has ever started. Ayaka — Dylan — draws the Kamisato blade in the long clean line she has drawn it in for sixty‑four years. The crowd around you ripples backwards in confusion, still mind‑hacked into worshipping you, suddenly seeing weapons in their heroes' hands.

Dottore does not move.

He sighs — politely — and lifts a finger.

The arrow that Brian F. fired never hits him. You don't see what happens to it; it's just gone, mid‑air, six feet in front of the half‑mask. Brian C.'s next step doesn't connect with anything either — the ground in front of him is suddenly not where he expected it to be, and he stumbles. And Ayaka —

(Pause. Look at Dylan.)

(Slide the black d20 across the table.)

Dylan — roll the d20 in front of you.

This is a death roll. There is no modifier. There is no bonus. Ayaka has committed to a strike against the second‑most dangerous person in this nation, and the only thing between her sword and his neck is the number on the die.

ResultHow it lands
All three pick the same personThat NPC notices something in the air — the party's body language shifted. Persistent impact: Whichever way they go, the missing Village Keepers are next session's main quest. The boy and the stone are the hook.

(Whatever they choose, the scene ends here. Lights low. Uncle Anpu thanks them again. Candace shows them to the guest rooms. Dehya cheers and goes to find an old friend at the well. Alhaitham, if he is with them, settles by a window with the Divine Knowledge Capsule in his palm and turns it slowly, in the lamplight. Cyno does not go to a guest room — he sits cross‑legged in the corner of the main room, spear across his knees, and does not move all night.)

(Outside, the desert is quiet. The wind has stopped completely.)

(Somewhere very far away, in a place none of you can see, Nahida opens her eyes inside a glass tank, looks up at a ceiling she does not recognize, and quietly recites the first three lines of a Sumeran poem about a moonless night.)

(End session.)

If You Run Short

Recommended cut order, from least to most painful:

  • Cut Scene 8 wave 1 (the Stone Enchanters). Run only the Loremaster and the Sunfrost. Saves ~10 min, still keeps the surprise drop and Dehya's revenge beat.
  • Cut Scene 13 wave 1. Run only wave 2 with Dehya alongside. Saves ~10 min, still gets the desert/Ruin Drake moment.
  • Compress Scene 2 to two acquaintances — skip Akim, run Qishan and Rima. Saves ~5 min, still gets the "report" reveal and the Rima break.
  • Cut Alhaitham's Caravan Ribat speech (Scene 9) to one line: "I'm still studying the capsule. I want to come with you to Aaru." Saves ~5 min. Roll the choice immediately.
  • (Last resort) Skip Scene 1 (Dawn debrief) — open the session cold on Nahida's pitch about Setaria. Saves ~5 min but loses the Dunyarzad‑alive contrast that makes the rest of the night ache.

If You Have Extra Time

  • Expand Scene 2 with a fourth acquaintance. Nabiya the bookseller — an old friend Setaria left a letter unanswered with. Use her to plant the "letters from the children of my homeland" grief beat that makes Rima's appeal land harder.
  • Have Dottore taunt them through the mob in Scene 5. If the party tries to stall, let Dottore answer one of their questions politely — what is in the Divine Knowledge Capsule, who the "external co‑lead" of Mass Dream Harvest is, why Scaramouche is in Sumeru. Pick one, give one truth. They will remember.
  • Insert a small road encounter in Scene 7 — one Eremite scout the party catches before Tighnari does, allowing one of them to do something heroic between the Sumeru gate and the canyon ambush.
  • Run the Aaru date harvest mini‑game. A child invites the party to help pick dates from the high trees the morning after the cliffhanger. Use this as a slow scene — let them talk to Dehya, ask Candace about her ribbon‑spear, watch Alhaitham try to read a book under a tree while two children climb on him. This is one of the most beloved scenes in the game when DMs run it gently.
  • Give Cyno a TCG hand. If Brian C. or Dylan plays the Genius Invokation TCG, Cyno will accept a single match in the corner of the chief's house during downtime. He plays the same five‑card deck every time. He never loses, except by deliberately throwing.
