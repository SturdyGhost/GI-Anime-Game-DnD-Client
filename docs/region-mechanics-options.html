Fontaine

Bond of Life

Original mechanic: A status tracked as an HP-value "debt" that blocks all healing until cleared. Characters voluntarily take on Bond to power up attacks (damage scales with Bond amount), then clear it via healing (which pays off the debt instead of restoring HP). Core fantasy: blood magic — strongest when most vulnerable. Key characters: Arlecchino (slow burn DPS), Clorinde (rapid cycle), Sigewinne (support fuel).

Approach A — Faithful Adaptation

A1: Full HP-Value Bond Tracking BOND-A1

Bond of Life tracked as an exact HP number per character, just like the original game. Healing is intercepted and applied to Bond first. Damage bonuses scale proportionally to Bond value as a percentage of max HP.

Core Rules

  • Bond of Life = a number (0 to 2× max HP) tracked per battler alongside HP and shields
  • While Bond > 0: ALL incoming healing reduces Bond instead of restoring HP
  • Damage bonus = Bond ÷ Max HP × base multiplier (e.g., Bond at 65% max HP with 10× multiplier = +6.5 damage)
  • Specific abilities consume Bond: attacks reduce it by a set amount per hit, Bursts consume all Bond and heal for that value
  • Some characters (Arlecchino) flagged as "cannot receive ally healing" — only self-heal via Burst
  • Multiple Bond sources stack additively
  • New effect conditions: HAS_BOND, BOND_ABOVE_PERCENT, BOND_BELOW_PERCENT

Example Kit: Arlecchino Companion

  • Skill: Marks enemies with Blood-Debt Directives
  • Charged Attack near marked enemy: Absorb mark, gain Bond = 65% max HP (or 130% if mark aged 1+ turn)
  • While Bond > 30% max HP: Normal attacks convert to Pyro, deal bonus damage = Bond% × 0.1 per hit, each hit consumes 15% of current Bond
  • Burst: Consume ALL Bond, deal AoE Pyro damage, heal self for consumed Bond amount

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • Perfectly captures the original scaling fantasy
  • Arlecchino/Clorinde companions feel authentic — damage scales with commitment
  • Threshold effects (>30%, =100%) create real decision points each turn
  • Healing interception is intuitive: "your heals pay off debt first"
  • Works as both a self-buff AND an enemy debuff (Fatui Operatives can inflict it)

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • New per-character tracked value — currently you track HP, shields, burst charges, status effects. This adds another number
  • Percentage-based damage scaling means math at the table: "what's 65% of 42 HP?"
  • Healing interception is a new rule that touches ALL healing in the game when Bond is present
  • DM must remember to check Bond before applying any heal
  • Could slow down turns for Bond characters doing the scaling math
  • UI needs a Bond display element on character cards

A2: HP-Value Bond with Simplified Scaling BOND-A2

Same HP-value tracking as A1, but damage bonuses use a lookup table instead of percentage math. Bond value maps to a bonus tier just like stats map to dice in your combat system.

Core Rules

  • Bond tracked as exact HP number (same as A1)
  • Healing interception (same as A1)
  • Damage bonus uses a tier table based on Bond as % of max HP:
  • 1-25% → +1 damage | 26-50% → +2 | 51-75% → +3 | 76-100% → +4 | 101%+ → +5
  • No per-hit percentage math — just check which bracket you're in
  • Abilities still consume Bond by fixed amounts (e.g., "each attack reduces Bond by 5")

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • Still tracks exact Bond for faithful feel, but no multiplication at the table
  • Tier table matches existing design pattern (stat→dice table)
  • Thresholds are easy to eyeball: "I'm over 50% Bond so +3"
  • Arlecchino/Clorinde kits still feel scaling and distinct
  • DM can announce tier: "you're at High Bond, +4 damage"

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • Still tracking an exact HP number per character
  • Still need healing interception logic
  • Players need to know their max HP percentage bracket (minor mental math)
  • The tier jumps feel slightly less smooth than true scaling
  • UI still needs Bond tracking display

A3: Dice-Value Bond BOND-A3

Bond is tracked as an HP number, but instead of flat bonuses, your Bond value determines a bonus damage die using the existing stat→dice table. Bond of 8 HP means you roll a bonus D8 on attacks. Fits the existing dice-driven combat identity.

Core Rules

  • Bond tracked as HP number (same as A1/A2)
  • Healing interception (same as A1/A2)
  • While Bond > 0: attacks gain a bonus damage die determined by Bond value on the stat→dice table (Bond of 6 = bonus D6, Bond of 12 = bonus D12)
  • Each attack reduces Bond by the value rolled on the bonus die
  • Burst: consume all Bond, heal self for that amount, deal damage = Bond value
  • Natural synergy: higher Bond = bigger bonus die = faster consumption. Self-balancing.

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • Uses the dice table players already know — zero new math concepts
  • Rolling bonus dice is FUN and tactile at a tabletop
  • Self-balancing: big Bond = big die = drains faster. No runaway states
  • Consumption is organic (you roll what you spend) instead of fixed decrements
  • Feels like the existing combat system extended, not a foreign mechanic bolted on

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • Departs from original Genshin math — Bond doesn't work as a "multiplier" like in-game
  • Still tracking exact Bond HP value
  • Rapid Bond consumption via big dice might make the buff feel too short
  • Clorinde's "build to 100% for max damage" loop is harder to capture — she wants to accumulate, not spend per-hit
  • Need to decide: does the bonus die replace or add to the normal damage die?

Approach B — Simplified Homebrew

B1: Binary Unhealable + Flat Damage Buff BOND-B1

No Bond tracking at all. Characters gain an "Unhealable" status effect with a duration, paired with a flat damage bonus while active. Simple on/off toggle.

Core Rules

  • Abilities that would grant Bond instead apply "Unhealable" status (2-3 turns) + flat damage buff (+3 or +4)
  • While Unhealable: all healing to this character is blocked entirely (not redirected — just blocked)
  • Burst/special ability removes Unhealable early and heals for a set amount
  • No scaling, no tiers — damage bonus is the same whether you just got it or it's about to expire
  • Implemented entirely through existing effects: WHEN: ON_SKILL_USE, DO: APPLY_STATUS "Unhealable" + STAT_BONUS, FOR: 3

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • Zero new systems — uses existing status effects and damage bonuses
  • Trivial to implement: just a new status effect name and a healing check
  • Players understand instantly: "you can't heal but you hit harder"
  • No math, no tracking, no new UI elements
  • DM doesn't need to remember anything new — it's just another status effect

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • Completely loses the scaling fantasy — no "I'm at 130% Bond and hitting like a truck"
  • Arlecchino and Clorinde feel identical: both just get a flat buff + can't heal
  • No decision-making: you don't choose when to cash out, it just expires
  • The "blood magic risk/reward" is barely present — it's just a buff with a downside
  • Artifact sets like Fragment of Harmonic Whimsy lose their "fluctuation" trigger since there's nothing to fluctuate
  • Players who know Genshin will notice how watered down it is

B2: HP Sacrifice Mechanic BOND-B2

Instead of Bond blocking healing, characters spend HP directly to power up attacks. No healing block — you're literally paying health for damage. Closer to "blood magic" in traditional RPG terms.

Core Rules

  • Abilities that would grant Bond instead cost HP to activate: "Spend 5 HP to deal +4 damage on your next 2 attacks"
  • No healing restriction — you CAN heal, but you're already down the HP
  • Burst abilities deal bonus damage based on missing HP: "+1 damage per 5 HP below max"
  • Creates natural tension: spend HP for offense, but you're closer to death
  • Healing is still useful (recovers the HP you spent) but doesn't negate the mechanic

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • HP is already tracked — no new resources needed
  • Extremely intuitive RPG mechanic: "spend health to hit harder"
  • Creates genuine risk: you can die if you over-invest
  • Missing HP scaling on Bursts gives a satisfying "desperation attack" moment
  • Healers on the team interact naturally — they restore what you spent
  • Works well with the existing damage formula (additive bonus from HP cost)

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • Not really Bond of Life at all — it's a different mechanic with similar flavor
  • Loses the "healing is blocked" tension entirely — that's the core of Bond
  • Arlecchino's "can't be healed by allies" doesn't translate (she CAN be healed here)
  • Fragment of Harmonic Whimsy (fluctuation bonus) doesn't map cleanly
  • Enemies inflicting "Bond" as a debuff doesn't work — they'd be dealing damage, not applying a status
  • Could be too punishing if HP pools are small — spending 5 HP when you have 30 is very different from Genshin's 30k HP characters

B3: Reckless Stance Toggle BOND-B3

Bond characters have a stance toggle: Defensive (normal) or Reckless (Bond active). While Reckless: +3 damage on all attacks, but take +2 damage from all sources and can't be healed. Toggle back as a free action on your turn.

Core Rules

  • Bond characters get a "Reckless Stance" they can toggle as a free action at the start of their turn
  • Reckless: +3 damage dealt, +2 damage taken, healing blocked
  • Defensive: normal stats, healing works
  • Burst abilities have bonus effects when used from Reckless stance (extra damage, self-heal on exit)
  • Some characters (Arlecchino) are always Reckless — can't toggle to Defensive. Burst is their only healing.

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • Binary state — trivially simple to track (a flag, not a number)
  • The +damage taken adds tension that flat "Unhealable" (B1) lacks
  • Toggle mechanic gives players agency each turn: "am I safe enough to stay Reckless?"
  • Arlecchino's "permanently committed" variant feels distinct from toggle characters
  • No new resources, just a state flag per character

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • No scaling — Reckless is always the same bonus, no "deeper commitment = bigger reward"
  • Clorinde's rapid build-and-spend loop doesn't exist — she just toggles
  • The +2 damage taken is a homebrew addition that doesn't match original Bond (Bond doesn't make you take more damage)
  • Sigewinne's "Bond fuels my healing" archetype can't work with a binary toggle
  • Artifact/weapon effects that key off Bond fluctuation don't apply

Approach C — Hybrid

C1: Three-Tier Bond (None / Low / High) BOND-C1

Bond tracked as three simple tiers instead of exact HP. Each tier gives a set damage bonus and blocks healing. Actions move you between tiers. Simple enough for the table, rich enough for distinct kits.

Core Rules

  • Bond tiers: None (no Bond) → Low (+2 damage, healing blocked) → High (+4 damage, healing blocked)
  • Abilities grant Bond at a specific tier: "Skill grants High Bond" or "each attack builds one tier"
  • Attacks can consume tiers: "each attack drops Bond by one tier" or "special attack consumes all Bond"
  • Healing action while Bond > None: clears one tier (healing consumed, no HP restored)
  • Burst effects can key off current tier: "at High Bond, Burst deals +6 extra"

Kit Differentiation

  • Arlecchino: Skill → High Bond. Attacks at High = +4, each attack drops a tier. Burst at any Bond tier = consume all + heal + bonus damage per tier consumed.
  • Clorinde: Attacks build Bond (None→Low→High). Special attack at High = max damage + drop to None + self-heal. Rapid cycling.
  • Sigewinne: Skill gives Low Bond. Clearing Bond gives +1 burst charge. While she has Bond, her heals to allies are +2.

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • Three states is trivially trackable — a marker on the character card or a mental note
  • Keeps meaningful scaling (Low vs High matters) without exact math
  • All three character archetypes (Arlecchino/Clorinde/Sigewinne) feel distinct
  • Healing interception still exists but is simpler: "heal clears one tier"
  • Artifact effects can key off tier changes: "when Bond tier changes, +3 damage for 1 turn" (Fragment of Harmonic Whimsy)
  • Easy to announce: "I'm at High Bond" — everyone knows what that means
  • DM can inflict Bond on players via enemies: "Fatui Operative hits you — gain Low Bond"

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • Less granular than original — no difference between "barely above 30%" and "at 130%"
  • Three tiers might not be enough for complex kits (could add a third "Critical" tier if needed)
  • Still need a healing interception rule (but simpler than A1)
  • New enum to track per character (though much simpler than an HP value)

C2: Bond Points (Small Integer Pool) BOND-C2

Bond tracked as 0-10 Bond Points (not HP values). A small integer pool that's easy to count. Damage bonus = Bond Points ÷ 2 (rounded down). Healing clears Bond Points at a 1:1 rate with healing value.

Core Rules

  • Bond Points: 0-10 scale per character
  • Abilities grant fixed Bond Points: "Skill grants 6 Bond Points" / "each attack grants 2 Bond Points"
  • Damage bonus while Bond > 0: +1 per 2 Bond Points (so 6 Bond = +3 damage)
  • Healing blocked while Bond > 0. Each point of healing clears 1 Bond Point instead.
  • Attacks can consume Bond Points: "this attack costs 3 Bond Points for +5 damage"
  • Burst: consume all Bond Points, heal self for 2× points consumed, deal bonus = points consumed
  • Threshold effects: "while Bond ≥ 5: attacks convert to elemental"

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • More granular than tiers (C1) but simpler than HP tracking (A1)
  • 0-10 is easy to count on fingers or track with a d10
  • Damage math is simple: divide by 2. Or just use a reference (5 Bond = +2, 8 Bond = +4)
  • Healing interaction is clean: 1 healing = 1 Bond cleared
  • Supports both "build up" (Clorinde) and "spend down" (Arlecchino) patterns
  • Threshold effects at specific point values (≥5, ≥8) create decision points
  • Can express in effects system: IF: BOND_POINTS >= 5, DO: ELEMENT_CONVERT

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • Still a new per-character resource to track (though small)
  • Abstracted away from HP — loses the "your health IS the resource" flavor
  • Healing clearing Bond at 1:1 means a 5-point heal clears 5 Bond — might drain too fast or slow depending on healing values
  • Need to tune the numbers carefully against your HP/healing scale

C3: Bond Die (Escalating Risk Die) BOND-C3

Bond is represented by a die that sits in front of the player. When you gain Bond, you get a Bond Die (starts at D4). Abilities can escalate the die (D4→D6→D8→D10→D12). On each attack, roll the Bond Die as bonus damage. On a 1, Bond breaks and healing is restored. High risk, high reward, very tactile.

Core Rules

  • Gaining Bond = place a Bond Die in front of you (starts at D4 or higher per ability)
  • While you have a Bond Die: healing is blocked, roll it as bonus damage on every attack
  • Some abilities escalate the die: D4→D6→D8→D10→D12 (bigger risk, bigger reward)
  • On any Bond Die roll of 1: Bond breaks. Die is removed. Healing restored. No bonus damage that hit.
  • Burst: voluntarily break Bond. Roll the Bond Die one final time as bonus damage + heal for the die's max value.
  • Arlecchino: starts at D8, escalates to D12 via Skill. Clorinde: starts at D4, escalates rapidly via attacks.

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • Incredibly tactile and fun at a physical table — the die IS the Bond
  • Escalation mechanic already exists in your system (Brian C.'s Nature Skill) — proven pattern
  • Self-balancing via the "roll 1 = break" rule. Bigger dice are harder to roll a 1 on (D12 = 8.3% chance vs D4 = 25%)
  • Creates genuine tension every roll: "please don't be a 1..."
  • No math — just roll the die. The die IS the tracking.
  • Players will LOVE the push-your-luck element

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • Significantly departs from original Bond of Life — this is more "cursed die" than "healing debt"
  • The random break on 1 removes player agency — in original, you CHOOSE when to clear Bond
  • Clorinde's "build to 100% then cash out" loop becomes "escalate die and hope it doesn't break"
  • Harder to implement in the digital client — needs a "Bond Die" display with escalation
  • Sigewinne's support Bond usage doesn't map well to a push-your-luck die
  • Enemy-inflicted Bond is weird: "here, take this cursed die"

🏆 Bond of Life — Recommended Ranking

#1
C1: Three-Tier Bond
Best balance of simplicity and depth. Three tiers are instantly graspable, keep all three character archetypes distinct, and integrate cleanly with the effects system. Healing interception is simple ("heal clears one tier"). The table overhead is minimal — just announce your tier. This is the sweet spot.
#2
C2: Bond Points (0-10)
More granular than tiers if you need it, and the 0-10 scale is very manageable. Good fallback if C1 feels too coarse. The main risk is tuning healing-to-Bond-clearing ratios against your existing healing values.
#3
A2: HP-Value with Lookup Table
If you want maximum faithfulness without the percentage math, this is the best faithful option. The tier table mirrors your existing stat→dice pattern. Only pick this if you really want exact HP tracking for narrative reasons.
#4
C3: Bond Die (Escalating Risk)
The most FUN option at a physical table — players will love the push-your-luck tension. But it departs significantly from original Bond and removes player agency over when to clear. Great if fun > accuracy. The escalation pattern already exists in Brian C.'s kit, proving the concept works.
#5
A3: Dice-Value Bond
Clever integration with existing dice system, and self-balancing. But the "roll what you spend" consumption model makes Bond feel too temporary for Arlecchino-type characters who want to sustain high Bond.
#6
B2: HP Sacrifice
Intuitive RPG mechanic but it's not really Bond of Life — it's a different thing with vaguely similar flavor. Pick this only if you want to abandon the Bond concept entirely and do your own "blood magic" take.
#7
A1: Full HP-Value Tracking
Maximum accuracy but the per-hit percentage math will slow down the table. Only worth it if you automate all Bond math in the client and never calculate by hand.
#8
B3: Reckless Stance Toggle
Clean and simple but too flat. The +damage taken addition isn't true to Bond and the binary toggle removes all scaling depth. Characters feel samey.
#9
B1: Binary Unhealable + Flat Buff
Too simple. Loses everything that makes Bond interesting. Only use this as a fallback if you decide Bond isn't worth ANY mechanical investment.
Natlan

Nightsoul's Blessing

Original mechanic: A transformation state fueled by personal Nightsoul Points (60-100 cap) that drain in real-time. Backed by Phlogiston (shared party pool of 100) when personal points run out. Characters gain enhanced/converted attacks while active. Nightsoul Burst is a periodic team proc (cooldown scales with Natlan character count) that triggers individual character bonuses. Mavuika gains Fighting Spirit when allies spend Nightsoul Points, fueling her Burst. Core fantasy: channeling ancestral power on a ticking clock.

Approach A — Faithful Adaptation

A1: Full Dual Resource + Burst Timer NS-A1

Translate everything: personal Nightsoul Points per character, shared Phlogiston pool, Nightsoul Burst on a round timer, Fighting Spirit for Mavuika. Maximum fidelity.

Core Rules

  • Each Natlan character has a Nightsoul Point pool (cap set per character: 60-100)
  • Skill activates Nightsoul's Blessing, fills points to max
  • While active: attacks are enhanced (element conversion, bonus damage, kit-specific effects)
  • Each turn costs Nightsoul Points (20/turn baseline, some abilities cost more)
  • When personal points = 0: spend Phlogiston instead (20/turn from party pool of 100)
  • When both empty: state ends
  • Nightsoul Burst: triggers every N rounds (1 Natlan char = 4 rounds, 2 = 3 rounds, 3+ = 2 rounds). Each character has a passive that fires on Burst.
  • Mavuika: gains Fighting Spirit = Nightsoul Points spent by allies. Burst requires 100+, consumes all.

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • Every Natlan character plays exactly as designed — full kit fidelity
  • Mavuika's "allies fuel me" loop works perfectly
  • Nightsoul Burst rewards team composition just like the original
  • Phlogiston as shared backup creates party-level resource decisions
  • Artifact sets (Obsidian Codex, Scroll) work exactly as intended

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • THREE new resources: personal points, party Phlogiston, Burst timer. This is the most bookkeeping of any option in this entire document.
  • Fighting Spirit is a FOURTH resource unique to one character
  • 60-100 point pools with 20/turn drain means tracking multi-digit numbers every turn
  • DM has to track a round counter for Nightsoul Burst AND the Phlogiston pool
  • Significantly more complex than anything else in your current combat system
  • Risk of slowing down combat as players calculate point costs

A2: Scaled-Down Faithful (Smaller Numbers) NS-A2

Same structure as A1 but with smaller numbers. Nightsoul Points cap at 10 (not 60-100). Phlogiston pool = 10 (not 100). Costs = 2-3/turn. Same mechanics, friendlier math.

Core Rules

  • Nightsoul Points: 0-10 per character. Skill fills to max.
  • Base cost: 2 points/turn. Enhanced abilities cost 3-4 points.
  • Phlogiston: party pool of 10. Costs 2/turn when personal points empty.
  • Nightsoul Burst: same round timer as A1
  • Mavuika: Fighting Spirit = total Nightsoul Points spent by allies (accumulates across battle). Burst requires 10+.
  • Everything else identical to A1 but with 1/10th the numbers

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • Same mechanical depth as A1 with much friendlier numbers
  • 0-10 is trackable on fingers, dice, or a simple counter
  • All kit interactions preserved

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • Still three separate resources + Fighting Spirit
  • Still need a Nightsoul Burst round counter
  • Smaller numbers mean less granularity for per-ability costs
  • Some kits that rely on spending different amounts lose nuance (e.g., "spend 30 vs 50 points" becomes "spend 3 vs 5")

A3: Client-Managed Faithful NS-A3

Full faithful mechanics (like A1) but the client handles all tracking. Players and DM don't manually count points — the app shows Nightsoul meters, auto-decrements, tracks Phlogiston, and fires Nightsoul Burst automatically. The complexity lives in code, not at the table.

Core Rules

  • Same rules as A1, but all resource tracking is automated in the battle client
  • Character cards show a Nightsoul meter that drains each turn
  • Phlogiston shown as a party-wide bar
  • Nightsoul Burst fires automatically on the round timer with a visual notification
  • Players only need to choose: "do I use my enhanced attack (costs more points) or normal attack?"
  • DM sees all resources on their host view

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • Full mechanical depth with zero table bookkeeping — the app does it all
  • Players just make decisions, app tracks consequences
  • This is what the client is FOR — state tracking that's tedious by hand
  • Nightsoul Burst auto-fire means DM never forgets
  • Visual meters make the resource drain dramatic and visible

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • Significant development effort — new UI elements, auto-decrement logic, burst timer system
  • Players might not understand WHY things are happening if the app is too opaque
  • If the client crashes mid-combat, you lose all the Nightsoul state
  • The DM loses some flexibility — auto-systems are harder to fudge on the fly
  • Development dependency: this mechanic is blocked until the client features are built

Approach B — Simplified Homebrew

B1: Timed Elemental Buff NS-B1

Nightsoul's Blessing is just a buff with a turn duration. Skill activates it for 3 turns. While active: +3 damage, attacks convert to element. No points, no Phlogiston, no Burst timer.

Core Rules

  • Skill: enter Nightsoul's Blessing for 3 turns
  • While active: +3 bonus damage, attacks become elemental, kit-specific enhanced effects
  • After 3 turns: blessing ends, back to normal
  • No team synergy mechanics — each character is independent
  • Mavuika: Burst charges gain +1 whenever an ally uses their Skill (simplified Fighting Spirit)

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • Zero new systems — just a status effect with a duration. Already supported.
  • Players understand "use Skill, get buffed for 3 turns"
  • No resources to track beyond the turn counter (which effects already handle)
  • Trivial to implement: WHEN: ON_SKILL_USE, DO: STAT_BONUS, FOR: 3

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • Nightsoul is DEFINED by resource management — remove that and it's just... a buff
  • No "do I spend big or conserve?" decisions. No Phlogiston party tension.
  • Every Natlan character plays identically: Skill → hit stuff for 3 turns → wait
  • Mavuika's "team fuels me" loop is reduced to generic burst charge gain
  • Nightsoul Burst team proc doesn't exist — no mono-Natlan incentive
  • Obsidian Codex ("after consuming 10 Nightsoul Points") can't work — there are no points
  • Kinich, Mualani, Chasca all lose their unique mechanics (grapple orbiting, surfing stacks, aerial absorption)

B2: Natlan Combo Counter NS-B2

Instead of individual resources, Natlan characters build a shared party Combo counter. Each Natlan character's attacks add to the Combo. At thresholds, the party gets bonuses. Mavuika cashes out the Combo for her Burst.

Core Rules

  • Shared "Natlan Combo" counter starts at 0 each combat
  • Every Natlan character's attack adds +1 to Combo. Skill attacks add +2.
  • Combo thresholds: 5 = all Natlan chars get +1 damage | 10 = +2 damage and +1 burst charge each | 15 = +3 damage
  • Mavuika: Burst consumes entire Combo counter, deals damage = Combo × 2
  • After Mavuika's Burst: Combo resets to 0
  • Nightsoul's Blessing flavor: Natlan Skills "enter the state" for 2 turns (enhanced attacks, element conversion) — this is what generates the Combo efficiently

✅ Pros vs Current System

  • Captures the team synergy fantasy — more Natlan characters = faster Combo = better payoff
  • Mavuika's "allies fuel me" is clean and exciting: watch the counter climb, then BURST
  • One shared number instead of per-character resources
  • Combo thresholds create natural momentum in combat
  • Easy to track: one counter visible to everyone

❌ Cons vs Current System

  • Individual Nightsoul resource management is gone — no "do I conserve points?" per character
  • Phlogiston doesn't exist in any form
  • Characters that spend Nightsoul Points in unique ways (Kinich's grapple, Mualani's stacks) lose identity
  • Combo is effectively "attack a lot and Mavuika nukes" — less nuanced than original
  • Non-Mavuika teams don't have a payoff for the Combo building

B3: Ancestral Surge (Per-Battle Transformation) NS-B3

Each Natlan character gets one "Ancestral Surge" per battle — a powerful multi-turn transformation. No point tracking. Once you use it, you're in the empowered state for a fixed window, then it's spent. Simple resource: you have it or you don't.

Core Rules
